# FSEGS LIG — Principes de gestion

**Semester:** S1  
**Attached engineering lesson:** 020 — Foundations Project: In-Memory Data Tool

## Scope

Organisation and enterprise concepts; stakeholders; objectives; resources; functions of the firm; management levels; planning/organising/directing/controlling; decision types; structure; authority/responsibility/delegation; coordination; environment; productivity/effectiveness/efficiency; basic performance indicators.

## Teaching notes

Management asks how an organisation mobilises scarce resources to achieve objectives. Distinguish effectiveness (meeting objectives) from efficiency (using resources economically).

A manager's decision can be strategic, tactical, or operational depending on time horizon and organisational level. Do not treat “manager” as a synonym for “leader”; they overlap but answer different questions.

An organisation should be described through objectives, actors, resources, activities, information, authority, and constraints. In case studies, state assumptions before proposing a solution.

## Worked case

A small retailer has rising stock-outs and long order-response times. Identify the objective, symptoms, measurable indicators, possible causes, decisions at operational/tactical/strategic levels, and one control loop.

## Exercises

1. Classify 12 statements as objectives, means, activities, indicators, or constraints.
2. Distinguish effectiveness from efficiency in four mini-cases.
3. Identify stakeholder interests in a small e-commerce firm.
4. Build a simple organisational structure for a 20-person company.
5. Distinguish line authority, responsibility, and delegation in a case.
6. Classify decisions by management level and time horizon.
7. Propose KPIs for service quality, productivity, and stock management.
8. Analyse the external environment of a Tunisian SME using a structured framework.
9. Explain how information quality affects managerial decision-making.
10. Write a short recommendation that compares two operational policies using explicit criteria.

## Problem-solving lab

Given a one-page case with symptoms but no diagnosis, build a problem tree: facts → causes → consequences → measurable objective → alternatives → decision criteria → control indicators.

## Exam patterns

Definition plus distinction; case analysis; classification; short structured essay; KPI selection; organisation chart interpretation; decision-level identification.

## Mastery gate

Analyse a fresh organisation case in 30–40 minutes, with a clear problem definition, causes, alternatives, criteria, and indicators. Avoid vague statements such as “improve management”.
