# FSEGS LIG — Business Communication 2 (English)

**Semester:** S2  
**Attached engineering lesson:** 065 — Go HTTP, JSON, and SQL

## Scope

Technical/report writing; structured argument; describing systems; explaining data; presenting recommendations; incident communication; professional correspondence; meeting participation; concise business English; evidence-based writing.

## Teaching notes

A strong business communication answer has a visible purpose, a clear audience, relevant evidence, and a controlled structure. Separate facts from interpretation. Separate recommendation from justification. Avoid vague claims such as “the system is much better” when a measurable observation can be stated.

For reports, prefer a compact structure: context, method or evidence, findings, interpretation, recommendation, and conclusion. For incident communication, identify impact first, then known facts, current action, remaining risk, and next update. For meeting minutes, record decisions, owners, deadlines, and unresolved questions separately from general discussion.

Business English assessment is not a programming test. Technical knowledge is useful only when expressed clearly enough for the intended audience to understand and act on it.

## Worked practice

### Practice A — Incident note

Given an API outage affecting checkout, write a 120-word message for management. State impact, confirmed facts, mitigation, and next update. Do not invent a root cause before evidence exists.

### Practice B — Recommendation

Compare two database deployment approaches. State the decision criteria first, then give evidence for each criterion and one justified recommendation.

### Practice C — Data commentary

Given a small monthly sales table, write three observations, one interpretation, and one decision implication. Do not turn correlation into causation.

## Exercises

1. Write a 200-word technical report with method and result.
2. Explain an API or information system to a non-technical manager.
3. Write an incident notification with impact, facts, action, and next update.
4. Turn a raw data table into a short management summary.
5. Write a recommendation comparing two implementation choices.
6. Draft meeting minutes that separate decisions from discussion.
7. Rewrite a vague statement as a measurable requirement.
8. Produce a five-slide presentation outline from a case.
9. Edit for unsupported claims and ambiguous language.
10. Give a two-minute oral defence of a technical decision, then write the 150-word written version.

## Timed assessment

Complete three tasks in 60 minutes: a formal email, a 250-word report, and a short evidence-based recommendation. Reserve the final five minutes for grammar, structure, and unsupported-claim checks.

## Exam patterns

Formal email, short report, summary, comparison, recommendation, data commentary, oral-presentation planning.

## Mastery gate

Communicate one technical decision in three forms: professional email, 250-word report, and two-minute oral outline, with evidence clearly separated from interpretation. Complete the timed assessment without templates.
